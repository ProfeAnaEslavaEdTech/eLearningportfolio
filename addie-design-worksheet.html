⚖️ Compliance standards this training must satisfy

In regulated MedTech manufacturing a completed course is a controlled quality record: an auditor expects to see who was trained, on which version of which procedure, that they demonstrated competency, and that records are retained and current. That's why this worksheet insists on objective↔source↔assessment traceability, version-locking, and a real mastery gate.

DPhase 2Design▾
What & why. Design turns tasks into measurable objectives, chooses the tool and interaction logic, and plans the assessment before any screen is built — so the course actually proves the objective.
  • Objectives: write them Mager-style — Performance + Condition + Criterion. “Understand” is not an objective; “select the correct kit with zero errors” is.
  • Design the measurement with the content, never after.
📐 How to write a learning objective for technical material

An objective states what the learner will be able to do after training — not what the course will "cover." It's the contract between content and assessment: it must be measurable, because what you can't observe you can't certify.

❌ Not an objective: "Understand the spill SOP" — unobservable, no standard.
✅ "Given a spill and the available kits, select the correct kit with zero errors."

Two tools, used together

Bloom's taxonomy sets the cognitive level (how deep is the thinking?). Mager (ABCD) sets the structure so it's measurable (how do I write it observably?). Workflow: Bloom to choose the level → Mager to write it.

Mager format — the structure

PartMeaningExample
ConditionSituation, tools, constraints given"Given a simulated spill and the available kits…"
BehaviorAn observable action verb"…performs all seven SOP steps in order…"
CriterionThe standard of success"…with zero critical errors."

Rule: the verb must be observable (do, select, assemble, calibrate, sequence), never internal (know, understand, be aware).

Bloom's taxonomy — the level

LevelUse when material requires…Sample verbsTechnical use
RememberRecalling facts, codes, limitslist, label, identify, recallName PPE items, hazard symbols
UnderstandExplaining meaningdescribe, explain, classifyExplain why water-reactive reagents need a dry kit
ApplyExecuting a procedure in contextperform, use, operate, executeRun the SOP; don PPE; contain a spill
AnalyzeJudging among options / diagnosingdetermine, differentiate, select, troubleshootChoose the correct kit for a reagent class
EvaluateJudging adequacy against criteriaassess, verify, justify, prioritizeDecide if an area is safe to re-enter
CreateProducing something newdesign, formulate, developWrite a new local spill procedure

For adult, simulation-based compliance training, objectives usually sit at Apply (do the procedure) and Analyze/Evaluate (choose right among plausible wrong). Remember/Understand alone are too low to certify competence.

Let the material drive the level (analysis first)

Derive the level from your job/task analysis + DIF, don't guess it:

  • Step is recall only → Remember/Understand → maybe a job aid, not a full objective.
  • Step is do the procedure → Apply → objective + performance assessment.
  • Step needs judgment/diagnosis → Analyze/Evaluate → objective + decision/branching item.
  • Match the criterion to risk: critical steps → zero-error (100%); non-critical recall → a pass mark (e.g. 80%).

Principle: the higher the stakes and judgment, the higher the Bloom level and the stricter the Mager criterion.

Terminal vs. enabling

Terminal = the whole performance at the end (one per module). Enabling = the sub-skills that build to it (ideally one per critical task), each mapping 1:1 to an assessment item — your traceability backbone (objective ↔ source clause ↔ assessment item).

Evaluate: verify assess justify prioritize confirm
